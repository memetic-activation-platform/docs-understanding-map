# The Five-Layer Model of Memetic Coordination in MAP

This model explains how the MAP enables coherence at planetary scale without domination. It does so by separating the evolution of meaning from the formation of commitments and from the emergence of coordinated action.

Each layer performs a distinct function in the ecosystem.

1. Meme Stewardship — the evolution of meaning
2. LifeCode Binding — the adoption of meaning into identity and governance
3. Compatibility Mapping — translation between meaning systems
4. Promise Matching — negotiation of commitments
5. Promise Weave Emergence — coordinated action networks

Together these layers form a pipeline from cultural diversity to operational coherence.

---

# Layer 1 — Meme Stewardship
### The Evolution of Meaning

At the base of the system is the Global Meme Pool: a federated network of stewarded knowledge commons where ideas, principles, values, governance models, and coordination patterns are curated and evolved.

Memes in the pool are:

- immutable once defined
- versioned when meaning evolves
- stewarded by communities that care for their clarity and integrity
- connected through semantic relationships

This layer supports:

- plural definitions
- conceptual experimentation
- competing interpretations
- forkable knowledge structures

Meaning does not require global agreement. Instead, it evolves through stewardship, experimentation, and adoption.

Key dynamic:

Many definitions may coexist.

Example:

Regenerative  
Regenerative v2  
Regenerative (bioregional definition)  
Regenerative (permaculture definition)

The meme pool preserves this diversity rather than forcing early convergence.

Function of this layer:

Maintain a pluralistic commons of meaning.

---

# Layer 2 — LifeCode Binding
### Meaning Becomes Identity and Governance

A LifeCode is where abstract memes become operational.

A LifeCode is a holon that binds an agent or group to specific memes representing:

- values
- principles
- goals and intentions
- governance structures

These bindings reference specific versions of memes.

Example:

LifeCode A  
VALUE → Regenerative v3  
PRINCIPLE → Prosocial Core Design Principles v5  
GOVERNANCE → Sociocracy variant v2

Crucially:

LifeCodes do not copy definitions.  
They reference them.

This creates two important properties:

1. semantic accountability
2. version clarity

When an agent says they value regeneration, the LifeCode specifies exactly which definition they are committing to.

Definitional relationships within a LifeCode form part of its identity. If these relationships change, a new LifeCode version is created.

Function of this layer:

Translate meaning into explicit identity, governance, and intention.

---

# Layer 3 — Compatibility Mapping
### Translation Between Meaning Systems

Because many definitions coexist, interoperability requires mapping between them.

Compatibility relationships between memes may include:

- equivalent_to
- overlaps_with
- broader_than
- narrower_than
- derived_from

Example:

Regenerative v3  
OVERLAPS_WITH  
Regenerative (bioregional definition)

Or:

Prosocial Principles v5  
DERIVED_FROM  
Prosocial Principles v4

These mappings can themselves be stewarded, versioned, and debated.

Mapping enables coordination without forcing conceptual uniformity.

Two agents may retain different definitions while agreeing on the relationship between them.

This implements ontological commoning:

Private meaning is preserved.  
Shared understanding emerges through mapping.

Function of this layer:

Create translation bridges across semantic ecosystems.

---

# Layer 4 — Promise Matching
### Negotiation of Commitments

Meaning becomes actionable when agents form Promises, Offers, and Agreements.

When an agent issues an inquiry or offer, they may reference elements of their LifeCode.

Example:

Inquiry requires alignment with:

VALUE → Regenerative v3  
PRINCIPLE → Prosocial Principles v5

Another agent responding may reference:

VALUE → Regenerative (bioregional definition)

Compatibility is determined through:

- exact version match
- mapped equivalence
- acceptable overlap
- fuzzy compatibility scoring

This process determines whether two agents can form an Agreement.

The key insight:

Agents are not required to share identical meanings.

They must only reach sufficient compatibility for the specific coordination context.

Function of this layer:

Determine whether commitments can be formed across semantic differences.

---

# Layer 5 — Promise Weave Emergence
### Coordinated Action Networks

When compatible promises accumulate, they form Promise Weaves.

A Promise Weave is an emergent network of agents whose commitments align sufficiently to support coordinated action.

Over time these weaves may evolve into:

- projects
- organizations
- commons
- agent spaces
- bioregional networks

The weave becomes the substrate for:

- governance
- resource flows
- shared decision-making
- collective action

At this stage, meaning has moved from theory into lived practice.

Function of this layer:

Enable self-organizing systems of cooperation.

---

# The Evolutionary Feedback Loop

These five layers form a continuous evolutionary cycle.

Meaning evolves in the meme pool.

LifeCodes bind agents to particular meanings.

Promise matching tests whether those meanings are compatible in practice.

Successful coordination produces promise weaves.

Successful weaves influence which memes gain adoption.

The loop can be expressed as:

Meaning diversity  
→ Identity commitments  
→ Compatibility negotiation  
→ Coordinated action  
→ Memetic selection

Ideas that support successful coordination tend to spread.

Ideas that do not support effective cooperation tend to fade.

This creates an evolutionary pressure toward practical coherence rather than ideological uniformity.

# Why This Solves the Core Coordination Problem

Traditional large-scale coordination relies on:

central authority  
standardized definitions  
institutional enforcement

The MAP model replaces those mechanisms with:

plural semantic commons  
explicit identity commitments  
semantic mapping  
negotiated compatibility  
emergent cooperation networks

Coherence emerges through successful collaboration rather than imposed agreement.

Agents retain sovereignty over their meanings.

Yet collective action becomes possible.

# The Core Insight

The MAP does not attempt to force agreement about meaning.

Instead, it builds infrastructure where:

meaning can remain diverse  
commitments can be explicit  
compatibility can be negotiated  
coordination can emerge

In other words:

diversity of worldview is preserved  
while coherence of action becomes possible.
